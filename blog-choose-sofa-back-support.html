How to Choose a Sofa That Actually Supports Your Back

September 28, 2026 · 6 min read · by Dr. Ellen Marsh, Ergonomics Advisor

A beautiful sofa that leaves you aching after twenty minutes is not a bargain — it's a daily tax on your comfort. After advising FurnitureShow on ergonomics for five years, I've learned that back support comes down to three measurements most shoppers never check.

1. Seat depth: 20–22 inches for most adults

Sit all the way back. If your knees bend comfortably with feet flat on the floor and 2–3 fingers of space behind your knees, the depth is right. Deeper than 24 inches looks luxurious but pushes shorter sitters into a slump. Taller than 6'2"? Look for 23–24 inches.

2. Cushion firmness: medium-firm wins

Feather-down feels wonderful for ten minutes and terrible by evening. High-resilience foam rated 2.4–2.8 lb per cubic foot holds your pelvis in neutral without feeling like a bench. Our Aurora sofa uses exactly this density — it's the most common reason customers cite in five-star reviews.

3. Back height: at least 18 inches above the seat

Your shoulder blades need somewhere to rest. Low-profile sofas look sleek in photos, but anything under 16 inches of back height transfers load to your lower spine. For seniors, a slightly higher back also makes standing dramatically easier.

One final tip: test the stand-up. A good sofa lets you rise without using your hands. If you need armrests to get up, the seat is too soft or too low — and that's exactly the kind of detail we publish on every FurnitureShow product page.
